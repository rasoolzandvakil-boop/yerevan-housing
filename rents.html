Yerevan · Rental market and short-term rentals

Spike, Correction, New Normal

Rents doubled to tripled within months of the 2022 Russian influx, gave back 30–40 percent as the relocants left, and settled 50–80 percent above pre-war levels. The Airbnb economics are workable but not spectacular — and supply is compounding faster than demand.

The rent arc

Sources: CivilNet; JAMnews; Mirror-Spectator; Numbeo (Aug 2026); spliced.

A central two-to-three-room apartment renting for AMD 140,000–180,000 (US$370–460) in January 2022 peaked at AMD 400,000–500,000 by late 2022, with landlords raising rents mid-lease in a market dominated by informal, unregistered contracts. From mid-2023 rents fell roughly 30–40 percent as most relocants moved on; Nagorno-Karabakh refugees partially offset the departures but at far lower purchasing power. By 2025–26 the market settled: rental transactions down 28.6 percent in 2025, a tenant shortage in the US$1,000–3,000 premium tier, the most-demanded bracket at US$300–500, and realtors' consensus that rents will not return to 2021 levels.

Current levels and yields

  • Central one-bedroom: US$550–850 (Numbeo listing average US$847 — skews to renovated stock); three-bedroom in the center ~US$1,320.
  • Outside the center: one-bedroom US$350–575; two-bedroom US$500–800.
  • Market practice: utilities tenant-paid; one-month deposits; discounts for 6–12-month prepayment; no rent control; leans landlord-friendly in practice, informal in form.
  • Gross yields: 5.5–7 percent in the center, 7–10 percent in Arabkir and the mid-ring; deduct ~1.5–2 points for the 10 percent tax, voids, and upkeep. The CBA governor notes the price-to-rent ratio is moving against investors.

The Airbnb picture

Yerevan's short-term-rental market is mid-sized, fast-growing on the supply side, and almost entirely amateur (0.2 percent professionally managed; ~88 percent international guests, Russia the top origin). The three analytics providers disagree on levels but agree on direction:

A well-run central one-bedroom grosses US$10,000–15,000 in a good year — against roughly US$8,000–10,000 on a long-term lease, a thin premium once cleaning, platform fees, utilities, management time, and voids are deducted. Seasonality is pronounced (August ~US$1,300–1,450/month vs. February ~US$730–780), stays are long (7.3 nights average — relocation and remote-work demand, not pure tourism). The demand side has plateaued: arrivals flat at 2.2–2.4 million since 2023 while listings grow 25 percent-plus — a recipe for rate compression, aggravated for dollar-earning hosts by the strong dram.

Tax and regulation

Taxation is light and identical across rental modes: a flat 10 percent on gross rental income for an unregistered individual (20 percent only above AMD 60 million/year), self-declared — platforms do not withhold. No STR permit, license, or zoning approval exists in Yerevan, and the tourism notification system effective July 2026 exempts private individuals letting a single apartment. Registered entrepreneurs can use a 10 percent turnover-tax regime. Property tax is moving to full cadastral market value from 2026 (0.05–1.5 percent). Fines apply for undeclared leases (up to AMD 500,000).
